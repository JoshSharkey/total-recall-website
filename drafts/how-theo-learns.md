Every action theo takes is logged and scored, so the agent is continually self-improving. This page explains the full loop, mechanism first, plain language beside it.

Most AI assistants remember what you tell them. theo also remembers what it *did*: every decision it makes while processing your signals, and how each one turned out. That record is called action memory, and it is why theo gets measurably better at being your assistant the longer you work together.

## 1. Decision traces

Every signal theo processes (a Slack thread, a sent email, a meeting, a calendar event) produces a decision trace: the signal that came in, the call theo made, the reasoning behind it, a confidence score, and, once known, the outcome.

In plain terms: theo keeps a logbook. Not just "what happened in your world" but "what I did about it, why, and whether that was right."

## 2. Retrieval of priors

Before acting on a recurring situation, theo retrieves its most similar past traces, the last N times it handled something of the same shape, along with how each one scored. The new decision is weighted toward the calls that were confirmed and away from the calls that were corrected.

In plain terms: theo rereads the logbook before making the same kind of call again. If assuming a quiet thread was "done" burned it twice, it stops assuming.

## 3. Outcome labeling

A trace is only useful once it has an outcome. theo gets outcomes two ways:

**Natural corrections.** When you say "no, that's not done" or "good catch" in normal conversation, that exact feedback becomes a permanent label on the decision it refers to. Confirmed decisions are reinforced. Corrections become lessons that block repeats.

**Automated reconciliation.** Daily and weekly sweeps verify theo's earlier predictions against what really happened, and score the traces nobody commented on.

In plain terms: there are no training rituals and no thumbs-up buttons. You react like a human, theo files it like a system, and the sweeps grade whatever you didn't.

## 4. Supersession: one live truth

When a fact changes (a new role, a new employer), the new value supersedes the old instead of accumulating beside it. Repeated observations that change nothing collapse into the existing record rather than piling up as duplicates. History is preserved, but answers are computed from the current, live version of each fact.

In plain terms: theo's memory doesn't hoard. Ask who someone works for and you get where they work now, not a stack of every employer they've ever had, and the old answer is still there if you ask for the history.

## 5. Recency decay, with per-outcome half-lives

Memory strength decays over time, and the rate depends on the outcome label:

- **Pending, unvalidated context** fades in about a month. If nothing ever confirmed it, it stops influencing decisions.
- **Confirmed knowledge** persists for about a year before it needs re-validation.
- **Corrections** stay warm for about six months. Lessons outlive noise.
- **Reinforcement resets the clock.** Every time a decision or fact is confirmed again, its decay timer starts over.

In plain terms: theo forgets the way you do, on purpose. Things that were never verified fade. Things you confirmed stick around. Mistakes you flagged stay vivid long enough to matter. And asking for "the latest X" actually returns the newest one.

## What this adds up to

The loop compounds: traces accumulate, labels sharpen them, decay clears the noise, and every decision starts from a better set of priors than the last one. This was verified by evaluation before shipping: retrieval quality measurably improved, with zero regressions.

In plain terms: nobody resets. Your one-sentence correction today prevents the same mistake next month, and the assistant you talk to next quarter is working from a quarter more evidence of what works for you.
