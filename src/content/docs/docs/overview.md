---# What the brain is (overview)

**In plain terms:** Total Recall is a private memory for one person. It quietly
collects the things you already deal with every day (your documents, notes,
emails, texts, meetings, the people you know, your health data) into one place,
and lets you ask questions about all of it in plain language. Think of it as a
second brain that never forgets and that you can talk to from your computer or
your phone.

It is **yours alone**. One brain belongs to one person. Your information is not
mixed with anyone else's.

## What it feels like to use

You ask, it answers, using everything it knows:

- "Summarize everything I know about Jane and her company."
- "What did we decide last week about the new pricing model?"
- "Pull the action items from my last call with the design team."
- "Whose birthday is coming up, and what should I get them?"
- "Find that note I wrote about the offsite idea."
- "Prep me before my 2pm."

You can ask from the Claude app on your phone, from Claude on your computer, or
through an assistant that messages you (like a Telegram bot).

## The two halves of the brain

It helps to picture two layers working together:

1. **Memory it builds for you (the "second brain").** In the background, it
   reads your documents, notes, emails, texts, and meetings and remembers the
   useful parts. This is the part that compounds: the more it sees, the better
   your answers get. Some of this runs only on an always-on computer (more on
   that in each capability page).

2. **Actions it can take right now (connectors).** Through Claude's own
   connectors, it can also do live things on your behalf, like draft an email,
   send a Slack, or check your calendar. These work from anywhere.

## What it is NOT

- It is **not** a replacement for your real tools. Your inbox, calendar, and
  files are still the source of truth. The brain makes you faster at reasoning
  over them; it does not own them.
- It is **not** a shared team wiki. It is a single-owner personal brain.
- It is **not** a magic box that knows everything on day one. An empty brain is
  not useful. The value comes from loading your real material at setup and
  feeding it over time.

## What makes a great setup

The brain shines when two things are true:

- **It is reachable when you need it** (including from your phone), which usually
  means it runs somewhere that is always on.
- **It is loaded with your real world** at setup (your notes, your contacts,
  your documents), so the very first question you ask returns something useful.
